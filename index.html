Biography

I am a first-year master's student at Peking University, co-supervised by Wentao Zhang and Weinan E at AAIS. I also work closely with Renrui Zhang.

Since June 2025, I have been a research intern at Kuaishou Kling AI. My published work is mainly on text-to-image generation. My focus now is robot learning, under one bet: frontier LLMs will become the brain of robots. They will act by writing and calling code. What robot builders must own is everything the model cannot ship: hardware, simulation, and deployment.

Opening the Physical World to AGI

The physical world was built for human hands and eyes. I want to rebuild its interfaces for agents. First, make robots and factories legible and callable: hardware as a command line, an "MCP for the physical world." Then close the loop: embodied agents that run experiments, repair their own hardware and production lines, and help build the next generation of themselves.

What I’m Thinking About

Three bets about how AGI enters the physical world. They are dated because I expect to revise them.

01

Frontier LLMs Will Be the Robot's Brain

Embodied AI is at its GPT-3 moment. I expect high-level reasoning and skill-level control (System 2 and System 1) to merge into one frontier model, instead of staying a separate robot-specific stack. Like computer use, the model layer of robotics then becomes winner-take-most.

What would change my mind: contact-rich manipulation, touch, and millisecond-level control. If specialized robot models keep a durable lead there while frontier models scale, "one model" goes less far than I think.

02

Code Is the Universal Representation

Code is compact, composable, and checkable, and it is what frontier models are best at.

For action: robotics is in a cold-start era with little action data. A policy that is written and verified in simulation starts from what the model already knows, not from zero. Simulation is to robot code what tests are to software: it makes the reward scalable and verifiable.

For generation: images, video, 3D, and interactive worlds can all be described as programs. If the reasoning moves into code, the generative model shrinks to a renderer. The open question is how light that renderer can be.

03

Hardware Is Part of the Algorithm

If the brain is shared, robot builders should build what is orthogonal to it: things that become more valuable with every model upgrade.

  1. A body the model wants to call. Design hardware backward from the task and the supervision you can get. Expose it as a clean command line: arms, touch, even the factory. A factory is also a robot.
  2. The best simulation of your own body and scene. Real-to-sim for the place you actually deploy.
  3. A deployment flywheel. Failures in the field flow back into simulation and data. Deployment scale is verification capacity.

Model, harness, and hardware are one system. Optimize them together.

Why the physical world at all. Coding advanced fast because its rewards are verifiable and its output feeds the next generation of models. I want the same loop in physical production. Compute must be physically built: fabs, power plants, data centers. Science moves at the speed of experiments, and today that speed is capped by human hands. Text and code give models their priors; the physical world gives them an open-ended curriculum and the final test. The goal is an AutoResearch loop for the physical world. Build robots that help build better robots.

Open Questions

  • How should reinforcement learning update System 0 and System 1/2 together, when today they are trained apart?
  • Model-based RL made locomotion efficient. Can it do the same for manipulation?
  • What actually blocks simulation at scale, and what keeps the sim-to-real gap open?
  • Does robot post-training need a very large model to generalize, given that deployment cannot afford one?
  • Are skills in the physical world multimodal?
