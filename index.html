Biography

I am an incoming master student at Peking University, where I am co-supervised by Wentao Zhang and Weinan E at AAIS. I also closely work with Renrui Zhang.

Since June 2025, I have been a research intern at Kuaishou Kling AI. Previously, my work mainly focused on text-to-image (T2I) generation. I am now most interested in World Action Models (WAM) and a broader question: how AGI can move from the digital world into the physical one. My long-term goal is to use AGI to build capable robots—and ultimately systems in which better intelligence creates better robots, while better robots expand our capacity for physical experimentation, production, and further intelligence.

AGI and the Physical World

I think not only about how AGI can serve people, but also about how it can improve the systems that create intelligence itself. Robots are the missing interface: they can turn intelligence into physical experimentation and production.

The Iteration Flywheel

Math and coding have progressed especially quickly because they provide scalable, verifiable rewards. Coding has an additional property: its product improves the process that produces the next product. Better coding agents help build better models, which in turn produce better coding agents.

I believe the most powerful products share this recursive structure. Their first user should be the company itself: every generation of the product should help the company build a better next generation.

My Long-Term Thesis

AGI helps us build better robots. Better robots expand the capacity for physical experimentation and production. That capacity then helps create still better intelligence and robots. In short: build robots that help build better robots.
